# Vermont Education Funding Watch — 2026-09-27

Covers everything published 2026-09-20 through 2026-09-27.

- THE SCHOOL CONSTRUCTION ADVISORY BOARD TELLS THE STATE BOARD IT CANNOT DO ITS JOB — the single most consequential item of the week, and it closes the open thread this watch has carried since the Sept 14 advisory board meeting whose agenda never posted. The State Board of Education's DRAFT MINUTES FOR SEPT 16, 2026 were posted this week and were read in full. Under agenda item (P), the State Aid for School Construction Advisory Board reported that it LACKS ADEQUATE EXPERTISE AND RESOURCES to complete the tasks assigned to it, and questioned whether it could "achieve the tasks that were assigned to them." The Board will meet in OCTOBER and NOVEMBER to try to meet its DEC 1, 2026 REPORT DEADLINE, and will ADVISE THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY about the resource constraints. This matters on this beat because that Dec 1 report is one of the two December deliverables (with the JFO consultant recommendations) that the 2027 legislative session is expected to build on, and because Act 170's construction-aid promise — up to $50 million annually in additional bonding capacity, and state support rising from 20-40% to 30-75% of qualifying projects — runs through this board. https://education.vermont.gov/sites/aoe/files/documents/edu-sbe-draft-minutes-09-16-2026.pdf (landing page: https://education.vermont.gov/documents/edu-state-board-meeting-final-draft-minutes-09162026 )

- THE $50 MILLION CONSTRUCTION-AID FIGURE HAS NO APPROPRIATED FUNDING SOURCE — Downs Rachlin Martin, "Vermont School Consolidation: Progress Update 09.21.2026" (Sept 21, 2026). SUBSTANTIVE CORRECTION-BY-SHARPENING of the Sept 13 entry, which logged Act 170 as envisioning "UP TO $50 MILLION ANNUALLY in additional state bonding capacity." DRM now reports that while Act 170 AUTHORIZES that capacity and the enhanced construction assistance, NO FUNDING SOURCE HAS BEEN APPROPRIATED for it. Read with the advisory board's own statement above, the construction-aid leg of the merger incentive is currently an authorization without money and without a board that says it can design it. DRM also notes the school construction PRIORITIZATION RULEMAKING is due MARCH 1, 2028 — immediately before the March 7, 2028 voter decisions, meaning districts would vote on mergers before the construction-aid rules they are being incentivized by are final. OTHER NEW ITEMS in this update: Lead Facilitator DAVID YOUNCE PLANS TO ISSUE STATEWIDE "GOOD FAITH" GUIDANCE BY END OF SEPTEMBER — this directly answers the open question logged Aug 31 and Sept 13 that no good-faith standard existed, with districts having asked for objective criteria, advance notice of violations, a chance to cure, and a review mechanism; ALL 13 REASSIGNMENT REQUESTS WERE GRANTED as of Sept 18 (the Sept 20 entry had the count but not that every one was approved); COMMITTEE VOTING STRUCTURE IS UNRESOLVED — committees will initially seat ONE REPRESENTATIVE PER DISTRICT and then decide whether to move to weighted voting by enrollment or proportional membership, which is a live governance question with direct funding consequences for small districts; MILTON launched a public survey on preferred merger partners and OTTER VALLEY is running community input sessions before formal negotiations. Standing caveat: law firm newsletter, reliable on public process, not a primary source. https://www.drm.com/articles/vermont-school-consolidation-progress-update-09-21-2026/

- LAKE REGION MERGER APPROVED UNANIMOUSLY — the new ORLEANS CENTRAL UNIFIED UNION SCHOOL DISTRICT. Closes the open thread carried since Sept 13 (agenda item R) and Sept 20 (no coverage of what the Board actually did). At its SEPT 16, 2026 meeting the State Board passed THREE UNANIMOUS MOTIONS: finding the merger of the Lake Region Union Elementary-Middle School District and the Lake Region Union High School District "in the best interests of the State, the students, and the school districts" (Werner/Kolbe); approving the study committee report and the articles of agreement (Werner/Samuelson); and assigning the new district to its own SUPERVISORY DISTRICT under 16 V.S.A. sec. 261 (Werner/Stuart). Superintendent Ramsey-Tolman will communicate the decision to the study committee. Note for the beat: this is a DISTRICT-INITIATED, VOLUNTARY merger completed OUTSIDE the Act 73/Act 170 facilitated process, and it is the first one this watch has seen reach approval — a useful baseline for what the 18 facilitated committees must produce by Sept 1, 2027. https://education.vermont.gov/sites/aoe/files/documents/edu-sbe-draft-minutes-09-16-2026.pdf

- THE ACT 170 QUALITY FRAMEWORK GETS A DRAFTING COMMITTEE, WITH AN OCTOBER DEADLINE — development on the Sept 20 entry, which read the framework deck and flagged that it set no thresholds, no per-pupil standards and no rulemaking dates. That is a concrete near-term date on the standards the 18 merger committees must satisfy, including the FINANCIAL SUSTAINABILITY dimension. https://education.vermont.gov/sites/aoe/files/documents/edu-sbe-draft-minutes-09-16-2026.pdf

- BARRE'S FOURTH BUDGET VOTE: OCT 20 OR OCT 27 FLOATED, CLERKS PUSH BACK AND WANT NOV 3 — Times Argus (Sept 24, 2026). Development on the Sept 20 entry, which logged that the board would meet the week of Sept 21 to set a date and that no date had been chosen. The board is now weighing OCT 20 or OCT 27, with the discussion CONTINUING OCT 5. Both the Barre Town and Barre City clerks objected to the compressed timeline: absentee ballot processing could not begin until after Oct 5, leaving too little time for overseas ballots, and clerk Lunt warned it would "unintentionally create barriers for voters who need to vote by absentee ballot." Both clerks would prefer NOV 3, alongside the general election, which would SAVE MONEY and avoid lining up poll workers for a fifth election in one year; Metivier warned the compressed schedule "would depress turnout." Still no date set, and the district continues on a line of credit at up to ~$22,000/month in interest while capped at 87% of the prior year's spending. https://www.timesargus.com/news/local/tight-timeline-floated-for-barre-budget-revote-has-clerks-concerned/article_a8cdcd14-2b8b-57c5-b9ea-16917897c73f.html

- BARRE'S BUDGET FAILURE IS NOW EXPLICITLY A MERGER PROBLEM — Vermont Public, "Vermont Edition" (Sept 21, 2026), with BUUSD Superintendent EMILIE KNISLEY, Vermont Principals Association executive director JAY NICHOLS, and reporter LOLA DUFFORT. NOT a duplicate of the Sept 20 Barre entries: what is new is the linkage, stated on the record by the superintendent's own district, between repeated budget failure and Act 170 consolidation. The program reports that a district has ALREADY VOTED TO REMOVE ITSELF from a merger committee that includes Barre, citing concern about how budget instability would affect future merger negotiations and operational planning. This watch logged the Harwood departure on Sept 20 as a geography-and-partner-preference move; the budget-instability motive is the new fact, and it means a district's inability to pass a budget now carries a cost in merger-partner terms as well as in line-of-credit interest. Framed as the recurring Vermont choice between raising property taxes and cutting school spending. https://www.vermontpublic.org/show/vermont-edition/2026-09-21/barres-school-budget-woes-could-affect-a-future-school-district-merger
