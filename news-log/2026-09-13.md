- Blue Mountain Union teachers declare IMPASSE, and the excess spending threshold number is now pinned down: 118% -> 115.5% — VTDigger (Sept 11, 2026) reports the Blue Mountain Union teachers' union formally declared impasse on Tuesday, Sept 10, 2026, rejecting the board's final offer of a ONE-YEAR contract after bargaining that began in September 2025. SUBSTANTIVE DEVELOPMENT on the story logged Sept 7 (VTDigger, Sept 4), not a duplicate: what is new is the impasse itself, the board's fallback to a one-year deal, the terms of the rejected three-year tentative agreement (raises of 4.75%, 5.5% and 6% in years one through three, on top of automatic step increases for education and experience), and the threshold trajectory — the excess spending threshold falls to 115.5% for the fiscal year beginning July 2027, DOWN FROM 118%. The Sept 7 entry had 115.5% but not the 118% baseline it is descending from; the size of the single-year step is the new fact. Board chair Kelsey Root-Winchester is named as citing the threshold and the local tax penalty it triggers (the Sept 4 story quoted board member Dan LaCoss). Next: a Vermont Labor Relations Board mediator enters, a process the story says takes months and costs both sides tens of thousands in legal fees. https://vtdigger.org/2026/09/11/teachers-in-blue-mountain-union-school-district-declare-impasse-in-contract-talks/

- FIRST HARD PER-DISTRICT FOUNDATION FORMULA LOSS FIGURES in this watch — Group 20 districts get numbers — The Bridge (Sept 8, 2026) reports that five districts in Act 73/Act 170 grouping 20 — Montpelier Roxbury Public Schools, Washington Central, Barre, Harwood and Paine Mountain — are forming a merger committee this fall to decide whether to put a merger to voters on TOWN MEETING DAY 2028. The funding content is the reason this is logged as core: the foundation payment base is reported as $15,033 PER PUPIL, adjusted annually for inflation; under the foundation formula MRPS projects a 3.2% DECREASE in state funding and WASHINGTON CENTRAL a 13% DECLINE, with Washington Central saying it would need $2-3 MILLION in cuts in the next budget season. Enrollment context given: Washington Central down 7.9% 2018-2024 and projected down 16.3% over ten years; MRPS projected down 12%. Foundation formula implementation is stated as JULY 2029. Until now this watch had the formula as a design question (JFO consultants, Dec 1 recommendations); this is the first time a district has put a percentage and a dollar figure on what it expects to lose. The Bridge says a follow-up on the other three Group 20 districts runs in its Sept 23 issue. https://thebridgevt.org/2026/09/local-school-districts-to-talk-merger-as-education-reform-kicks-in/

- The CONSTRUCTION-AID STAKES of "good faith" merger participation are quantified, and the good-faith standard still does not exist — Downs Rachlin Martin, "Vermont School Consolidation: Progress Update 09.07.2026" (Sept 7, 2026). Act 170 envisions UP TO $50 MILLION ANNUALLY in additional state bonding capacity for school construction, with state support rising from the current 20-40% debt assistance to 30-75% of qualifying projects where merger criteria are met — and districts found to be "BAD FAITH PARTICIPANTS" could LOSE construction-debt assistance. The lead facilitator is charged with identifying bad-faith districts, but DRM reports no standards have been developed for what good-faith participation requires, and no due process or appeal mechanism is defined. This is the money answer to the question the Harwood item raised on Sept 7 (whether bonding for consolidation could trigger state financial penalties): the penalty channel is construction-debt assistance, and the swing is large. Also reported: the facilitator team will moderate MORE THAN 20 merger discussions; the Legislature established 20 suggested groupings with facilitator discretion to modify; roughly 2,000 ADM is the minimum merged-entity threshold; Readsboro's school board voted unanimously Sept 3, 2026 to seek merger partners that preserve grades 9-12 school choice; Paine Mountain held public forums Sept 10 and 14 on consolidating grades 6-12 on one campus; Southwest Vermont CESA chair Rene Sanchez says CESA infrastructure buildout could take "six months or longer" (extends the CESA cost skepticism logged Sept 7). TIMELINE now given end to end: board appointments Sept 15, 2026; first committee meetings by Oct 15, 2026; lead facilitator status report Feb 1, 2027; merger committee recommendations Sept 1, 2027; State Board/AOE review Dec 2027; VOTER DECISIONS MARCH 2028. Same caveat as Aug 31: law firm newsletter, reliable reporting of public process but not a primary source. https://www.drm.com/articles/vermont-school-consolidation-progress-update-09-07-2026/
