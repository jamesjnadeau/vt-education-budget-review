# Vermont Education Funding Watch — 2026-08-30

Covers everything published 2026-08-28 through 2026-08-30.

- Barre opens the school year as Vermont's only district with no approved budget; third vote set for Sept 15 — VTDigger (Aug 30, 2026) reports Barre Unified Union School District started classes without voter approval after budgets failed in March and again in May. The board's third proposal is $57.1 million, a 2.1% increase over last year and $350,000 below the rejected May version. Tax impact splits sharply inside the single unified district: City of Barre homestead property tax rates would fall roughly 25-27% while Barre town rates rise, and about 61% of May's "no" votes came from Barre town. Operating without an approved budget, the district runs on a line of credit, may spend only 87% of the prior year's budget, and faces monthly interest of up to $22,000 to a local bank. It has already cut summer school, left vacant teaching positions unfilled and consolidated resources between schools; if Sept 15 fails, transportation, athletics and extracurriculars are the next targets. Superintendent Emilie Knisley says Barre spends less per pupil than neighboring Paine Mountain and Montpelier. Informational meeting Sept 8, 2026 at Spaulding High School. FIRST live school-budget-vote story in this watch, and the sharpest local illustration so far of what Act 73-era budget pressure plus divergent municipal tax impacts do inside one district. https://vtdigger.org/2026/08/30/schools-in-barre-start-the-year-without-a-district-budget/

Peter Welch presses a federal Farm to School Act with a stated Vermont school-meal funding angle (logged as ADJACENT, not core state funding policy) — VTDigger (Aug 28, 2026) reports Welch, working through the Senate Agriculture Committee as part of Farm Bill negotiations, introduced the Farm to School Act to expand local food purchasing in schools, building on the Farm to School Grant Program originally created by former Sen. Vermont context in the story: the state's Local Foods Incentive Grant pays schools up to 25 cents per meal when 25% of food is locally sourced. No proposed federal dollar figure and no Vermont fiscal impact were stated. The story also flags federal dietary guideline updates expected this fall (higher protein requirements, added-sugar limits) that districts will have to plan for.
