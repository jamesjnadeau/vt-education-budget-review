- SCHOOL HEALTH PREMIUMS PROJECTED TO RISE ONLY ~2.5% FOR FY28 — SMALLEST INCREASE SINCE 2009-10, BUT DISTRICTS SAY OTHER COSTS OFFSET IT — VTDigger (Nov 17, 2026, updated Nov 20; read in full) and Vermont Public (Nov 10, 2026; published in the prior window and missed, logged now; read in full). Vermont Education Health Initiative (VEHI, ~33,000 covered school employees and family members) expects average premiums +2.5% starting July 1, 2027, versus +11.9% for FY26 and 7.3% requested for FY27 (VSBA cites 16% for FY25); premiums are up more than 80% over seven years. VEHI expects to collect about $418M next year (~$10M more than this year); schools spend nearly $400M a year on premiums against more than $2B in education costs; employees pay ~20% under the statewide contract. Cost drivers cited: $44M saved over two years from price focus, $16M saved since Jan from limiting GLP-1 coverage, $5.3M from a new pharmacy benefit manager contract, more than $12M from a new BlueCross/UVM Health Network contract, plus the 2025 state drug-cost cap and Green Mountain Care Board hospital-budget limits (Mark Hage, VEHI trust manager). The rate still needs Department of Financial Regulation approval (usually January). Sue Ceglowski (VSBA) called it welcome relative to prior double digits; Diane Nichols-Fleming (Washington Central UUSD chair) called it "a huge break" but said diesel, heating fuel, food and building costs are harder to manage and the district faces a second tough budget year. Health benefits are now ~15% of school budgets, up from under 10% in 2018. Also notes that under H.949 (Act 169) the per-student spending threshold falls each year, with districts over it risking doubled taxes.
