- CONTENTS OF THE AUG 20 CLASS SIZE COMMITTEE MINUTES, now read in full. The user supplied the PDF directly, resolving the ROBOTS_DISALLOWED gap noted in the main Aug 25 entry above. - Meeting mechanics: SBE Minimum Class Size Implementation Committee, Aug 20, 2026, 1:02-2:15 p.m., virtual via MS Teams. SBE members present: Brian Campion (chair), Tom Lovett, Kate Webb (joined 1:06). AOE present: Cassidy Canzani, Dave Kelley, Emma Pearson, Annie Houston, Ian Burfoot-Rochford, Maureen Gaidys. Also Sarah Buxton (SBE Legal Counsel) and four field practitioners as invited witnesses. No public comment at either opportunity. June 1, 2026 draft minutes approved (Lovett moved, Campion seconded, passed).

- Purpose of the session: informational, not decisional — the committee heard AOE explain how class size data collection works and how the data is used, then took invited testimony from members of the Minimum Class Size Working Group on problems, guardrails and concepts ahead of applying the Act 73 Section 6 minimums.

- Unresolved definitional issues that drive the numbers: how to define and flag "terminal courses" (the last course in a sequence, e.g. French IV) — SBE is the body allowed to define this; the mechanical fact that offering more courses reduces average class size; each section crediting only one teacher; how special education enrollment is counted and coded; push-in and pull-out services likely not recorded as courses under Ed-Fi reporting; and an explicit caution from the discussion that "some of the numbers can be manipulated." Also discussed: whether monitoring sits in District Quality Standards or Education Quality Standards, personalized learning plans, and the Vermont Virtual Learning Collaborative.

- Governing document referenced: Secretary Saunders' 2/6/26 memo to the Working Group, "Working Definitions to Meet the Class Size Minimums Required by Act 73, Section 6 for the 2026-2027 School Year." Worth pulling separately — it is the operative definitions text.

- Field testimony, recurring asks: 3-to-4-year rolling averages rather than single-year counts; rotation of course offerings; "grace" for courses that need to adapt; and a hold-harmless clause. Matt Foster (Caledonia Central SU) noted Briggs Campbell led the Working Group. Andy Haas (Windham Northeast SU) warned that CTE students leaving for half days distorts class size counts and creates a disincentive to promoting CTE, and that AP and off-site learning affect counts too. Brenda Fleming (Rutland Northeast SU) said the rules "are going to affect high schools the most" and that CTE students in her district are out the entire day.

- DIRECT FUNDING LINK — the foundation formula: multiple witnesses tied class size minimums to the foundation formula work. Haas described himself as a proponent of the foundation formula because it addresses class size average and lets district leaders make choices based on values; Fleming said if the foundation formula comes to fruition "it will be the method of innovation that we have not had to date." This is the first evidence in this watch of practitioners publicly linking the Act 73 class size mandate to the pending foundation formula design — relevant to the JFO consultant recommendations due Dec 1, 2026.

- MOST SUBSTANTIVE ITEM — first on-the-record account of class size minimums cutting district staffing: Christine Bourne, Superintendent, Windsor Southeast SU, said her district worked to implement class size minimums this year and she "took a lot of heat" for it from teachers and boards; she struggled making cuts at the high school level and reduced some full-time staff to part-time to meet the requirements, which resulted in lost staff because those employees need full-time work. She also said she agrees there are classes that are too small and supports taking on the foundation formula. This is a concrete, attributable instance of the Act 73 mandate producing staff reductions — the kind of district-level cost effect this watch exists to catch, and a thread to follow for whether other SUs report the same.

- Next steps stated in the minutes: the committee aims to reconvene "in a few weeks" to hear from various education constituencies; before that, committee members will receive a document outlining the decision points they need to make to advance the work. NO DATE was set in the minutes — watch the AOE RSS feed and the committee event page for the next meeting notice, and for the decision-points document if it is published.

## Addendum 2 — working definitions memo obtained

- CONTENTS OF SECRETARY SAUNDERS' WORKING DEFINITIONS MEMO, now read in full. This closes follow-up (1) from the first addendum above. The user supplied the PDF directly; it remains unreachable to WebFetch. Document: MEMORANDUM, to Superintendents, Business Managers and School Board Members, from Zoie Saunders, Secretary, dated February 6, 2026, subject "Working Definitions to Meet the Class Size Minimums Required by Act 73, Section 6 for the 2026-2027 School Year," 6 pages. This is the operative definitions text districts are budgeting and staffing against for FY2027.

- STATUS AND LEGAL POSTURE: the memo states plainly that these working definitions "do not supplant the rule-making responsibilities of the State Board of Education, per Act 73, Section 8(a)." AOE issued the document as an immediate response to field requests for support and clarity while SU/SDs develop and finalize FY2027 budgets and staffing. Definitions were built with Superintendents, Business Managers and Data Managers to align with the existing End of Year Data Collection (DC04) and NCES SCED course codes.

- AOE ACKNOWLEDGES THE STATUTE'S TIMELINE IS BROKEN — notable admission, worth quoting: "The AOE and working group recognized that the timeline for the State Board of Education to establish final rules, the FY 27 budget development process and the initial year for these requirements (school year 2026-2027) were misaligned in Act 73." The working group says it sought to balance legislative intent with "a multi-year process marked by progress towards the class size requirements aligned to annual budget cycles." AOE further concedes some secondary-setting definitions may require revision once statewide graduation requirements are finalized through SBE rule-making. In plain terms: districts made FY2027 staffing decisions against definitions the agency itself expects to change.

- HOW THE AVERAGE IS CALCULATED (the mechanics that determine staffing): Act 73 amends 16 V.S.A. Sec. 165(a)(9) so that a school complies with AVERAGE class size minimum standards — an individual class may be smaller than the minimum average when minimums apply to content areas; "content area" means a group of courses within a specific licensing endorsement area. Method: within a single school, in a single year, a straight average of all non-exempt courses in a content area (students enrolled in content-area courses divided by total number of courses in that content area); for lower grades, total students in a grade over total classrooms for that grade. Content areas are standardized to SCED codes — core categories Math, English, Social Studies, Science, Arts (SCED 01-05); elementary uses SCED 2300x codes by grade level.

- MULTI-AGE CLASSROOMS AND STUDENT AGE: in a multi-age classroom the minimum becomes the LOWER grade's value — the memo's worked example is a 1st/2nd grade classroom taking the 1st grade minimum of 10 students, which also puts the 1st-grade minimum on the record at 10. Student age is not a factor: an 8th grader taking algebra at the high school counts toward the high school's math core average.

- RELATED ARTS ARE COUNTED, NOT EXEMPT: music and art (SCED 05), world languages (24), library (22003 Study Skills), PE/health/safety (08) and guidance (22999 Miscellaneous-Other) all count toward class size minimums. AOE flags that it and LEAs may need to work through "Miscellaneous" courses to determine whether any are exempt. This is a live cost pressure on small specialist programs.

- STATUTORY EXCLUSIONS, verbatim scope from 16 V.S.A. Sec. 165(a)(9)(vi): prekindergarten, kindergarten, career and technical education, flexible pathways, terminal courses, advanced placement courses, courses requiring specialized equipment, and driver's education are excluded. Small group services for special education, supplemental or targeted academic intervention, or English learner instruction are also excluded.

- "TERMINAL COURSE" — the definition the Aug 20 committee said SBE still has to settle: AOE's working definition is the highest level of a content area offered, the last one in a sequence, and it must meet BOTH tests — an advanced course last in a series AND a course "above" district graduation requirements. AOE states this definition will need revisiting once statewide graduation requirements are established, that districts must document which of their courses meet it for 2026-2027, and that "there will be some differences between districts." Not all honors classes qualify — only those last in sequence and above district graduation requirements.

- COMBINED HONORS/AP CLASSES — a trap for districts: although AP courses are exempt, in a combined Honors/AP class the AP students ARE included in the enrolled student count when calculating that content area's average. Advisory classes/homerooms (SCED 22 or 23) are exempt.

- "SPECIALIZED EQUIPMENT" working definition: highly specialized instructional equipment that is integral to the course curriculum (hands-on student use), requires dedicated instructional space or safety protocols, is not standard classroom furniture or basic supplies, and is used only in narrow subject areas. Examples AOE accepts: industrial CTE machines (welders, CNC, automotive lifts), advanced fabrication (3D printers, laser cutters), commercial kitchens and culinary equipment, media production gear, art kilns and ceramics wheels. Districts must document their own determinations; AOE again expects variation between districts.

- FLEXIBLE PATHWAYS: AOE signals updates will be required to align with its recommendations for statewide graduation requirements and federal ESEA compliance. EQS defines flexible pathways as including but not limited to CTE, virtual learning, work-based learning, service learning, internships, apprenticeships, community research, civic and community engagement, dual enrollment and early college, with learning supervised by an appropriately licensed educator. An "excluded courses" list is referenced as an attached file — NOT included in the PDF obtained; still outstanding. (Addendum 3 briefly recorded this attachment as superseded; addendum 4 retracts that — see below.)

- COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT: Act 73 Sections 6(b)-(c) set a multi-year accountability process — annual AOE data collections; a corrective action plan plus AOE technical assistance and support for districts out of compliance; actions AOE may recommend to the State Board; and SBE responsibility to ensure education quality standards are met. AOE, with the class size working group and SBE, will publish guidance on data collection, reporting and the accountability framework once SBE completes rule-making under Section 8(a). No financial penalty is described in this memo.

- KEY DATES — THE MOST OPERATIONALLY IMPORTANT PART: class size data will be collected through the annual End of Year data collection (DC04), so the FIRST collection of class size minimums takes place in SPRING 2027 and is reported in FALL 2027. But to support FY2028 budget development, AOE intends to provide districts with "baseline" class size averages from the 2025-2026 school year in FALL OF 2026, computed on these working definitions. That fall 2026 baseline is the first moment districts see their own numbers under the new rules, and it lands squarely in the FY2028 budget season — expect it to drive both district staffing decisions and public argument. Watch for it.

- ANALYSIS NOTE (clearly labeled as inference, not from the documents): the CTE complaint recorded in the Aug 20 minutes is consistent with, not contradicted by, the memo's CTE exclusion. CTE courses themselves are excluded from the calculation, but students who leave the building for CTE are absent from the non-exempt core courses that remain, thinning those sections and pulling the school's core content-area averages down. That is the mechanism Haas and Fleming were describing, and it is why they said high schools are hit hardest. Worth confirming against AOE guidance rather than treating as established.
